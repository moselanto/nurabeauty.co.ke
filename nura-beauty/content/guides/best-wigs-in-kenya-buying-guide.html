Step 1: choose the hair type

  • 100% human hair: the most natural look and movement. Can be washed, heat-styled and coloured. Costs the most and lasts the longest, about a year or more with good care.
  • Human-hair blend (semi-human): softer and more natural than fibre, cheaper than human hair. Low to medium heat only. About 6 to 9 months.
  • Heat-resistant fibre: the most affordable and holds its style straight out of the box. Low heat only. About 3 to 6 months.

Every NURA product name and label says which of the three it is, so you know exactly what you are paying for.

Step 2: check that "human hair" is really human hair

  • Price: compare with the ranges above. Far below them is the biggest warning sign.
  • Feel and shine: human hair has a soft, natural lustre; fibre is often very shiny or slippery and feels the same in both directions.
  • Burn test (a few hidden strands only): human hair burns, curls into a fine ash you can crush, and smells like burnt hair. Fibre melts into a hard, plastic-like bead and smells of plastic (The Standard, Evewoman).
  • Grades like 9A, 10A or 12A are not a standard. No industry body defines or certifies them; one seller's 10A is not the same as another's, so judge the hair, not the number (American Hair Loss Association).
  • "Remy" means the cuticles were kept aligned in one direction, which reduces tangling. It does not by itself mean the hair is unprocessed ("virgin").
  • Buy from a seller you can visit, with a physical address, clear hair-type labels and a written return policy.

Step 3: choose the construction

  • Headband wig: no lace and no glue; on in minutes. Best for beginners and busy days.
  • 2x2 or 4x4 closure wig: a small lace parting area, usually worn glueless. A good first lace wig.
  • Lace front (13x4 frontal): lace across the whole hairline for the most natural look; best fitted by a stylist. Costs more than a closure.
  • Full lace / 360: lace all round so you can wear high ponytails; the most expensive.

Step 4: choose texture, length and colour

Kinky straight (Yaki) and afro textures look closest to natural African hair; water curl, jerry curl and deep curl give volume; bobs and pixies are light and easy. Longer wigs cost more because they use more hair; a 20-22 inch human hair unit costs roughly twice a 12-14 inch one. Natural black (1B) and dark brown look the most natural; highlights such as 1B/27 or 1B/30 add warmth.

Step 5: budget for care and fitting

Plan for a sulphate-free shampoo and conditioner for human hair, a wide-tooth comb or detangling brush, and a wig stand.
